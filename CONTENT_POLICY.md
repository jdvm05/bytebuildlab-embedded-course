# Content, copyright, and brand-use policy

This policy covers this course repository and contributions to it. It is a
ByteBuildLab editorial policy, not legal clearance or a claim that every rule
below is required by law. Related videos, thumbnails, and other repositories
need their own review before publication.

## Original work and external references

- Write explanations and examples in our own words and create our own diagrams.
  Link the primary technical documentation used to verify a lesson. A technical
  reference is not permission to copy its text, artwork, or code.
- For copied or adapted material, record beside the material its exact source
  URL, author/rightsholder, version or access date, license/version, changes,
  and any required attribution. Preserve required notices. Retain written
  permission when permission, rather than a public license, authorizes reuse.
- Check permission for the intended distribution, modification, and commercial
  use, including use in a monetized course. A free download, search result,
  attribution alone, or an educational purpose does not establish permission.
  Copyright exceptions depend on the applicable law and circumstances; do not
  assume a blanket educational exemption for public tutorials.
- Link to datasheets and vendor guides instead of mirroring them by default.
  Review screenshots, product photos, diagrams, fonts, and icons separately.
  Taking a screenshot or redrawing an existing illustration does not by itself
  clear the underlying rights. AI-assisted creation is not proof of clearance.
- If the source or permission for proposed third-party material is unclear,
  use a link or independently create a replacement before adding it.

## Licenses and dependencies

Keep the root [MIT License](LICENSE) and its copyright/permission notice intact.
It covers our original code and written material as stated in the README; it
does not replace another owner's license or grant trademark rights.

Before importing code, libraries, SDKs, binaries, or media, check the exact
component's license and distribution obligations. Preserve its notices and
clearly identify any different license at its location. Do not relabel
third-party material as MIT. Share-alike, copyleft, noncommercial, and
proprietary terms require an explicit compatibility review for the intended
use; they are not interchangeable. Installing a tool separately is different
from distributing that tool or its components in this repository.

## Names, logos, and claims of affiliation

- Use product and organization names accurately to identify what is taught or
  required. Keep ByteBuildLab's identity clear. Descriptive references do not
  establish sponsorship, partnership, certification, or approval.
- As a house rule, do not add company, university, government, or certification
  logos to course branding, covers, badges, or certificates without a documented
  applicable permission. Necessary explanatory appearances in lesson material
  require a separate review of the relevant brand and content terms.
- Follow each owner's current guidance. Arduino's course guide allows
  descriptive name use but restricts using its logo to advertise a course.
  Its community-logo permission has a nonprofit condition that includes indirect
  income; do not assume a free-to-watch monetized video qualifies.
- Use requested first-mention symbols and ownership notices in reader-facing
  publications. The [README notices](README.md#trademark-notices-and-content-policy)
  accompany this repository; include relevant notices when exporting a page
  independently. Preserve exact code, filenames, URLs, package identifiers,
  and literal menu labels rather than inserting symbols into them.
- Do not describe our course as official or certified without evidence. It is
  fine to label a link to the manufacturer's documentation an official reference.
  Do not add registration symbols to ByteBuildLab without verifying registration.

## Before merging or publishing

Use a branch and pull request. The review should identify any external material,
its permission/license and notices, and any brand or affiliation claims. Put
source information next to the affected material; do not create a command log
or duplicate audit documents for routine changes. Recheck relevant owner terms
when introducing or changing their material.

If a credible rights concern arrives, preserve the identified content and
evidence, notify the maintainer, and review the specific claim. Do not contact
rights holders, admit liability, or submit a counter-notice automatically.
